Four business models wearing one word

They are all called “quant.” The risk, capital, latency and skill requirements barely overlap — and confusing them is the most expensive beginner error.

The highlighted row is the only one a solo operator can contest. Rows 1–2 are infrastructure races; row 3 needs LP capital and a risk department. Row 4's edge is behavioural and structural, not technological.

What the pod shops are really selling

Not signal quality — enforced loss limits. At Millennium the documented ladder is roughly a 5% drawdown from a portfolio manager's peak halving their capital and 7.5% ending the pod; Citadel and Point72 negotiate comparable limits per manager. Millennium runs 330+ independent teams, Point72 185+, and capital is reallocated daily, not quarterly. The headline Sharpe comes from many weakly-correlated books plus mechanical exits enforced by someone with no attachment to the thesis.

Both of those are copyable without any technology. That is the whole reason this page exists.

The equation that explains the industry

IR ≈ IC × √Breadth

Grinold's Fundamental Law: skill per bet × the square root of independent bets.

Medallion has a modest IC and astronomic breadth. A concentrated stock-picker has a large IC across ten bets a year and a mediocre IR. A 300-name universe rebalanced monthly looks like 3,600 bets but delivers a few hundred independent ones, because the names move together. The fastest way to raise your IR is not a cleverer signal — it is more genuinely independent bets.

The one structural advantage you have

Market impact scales with the square root of order size over daily volume. A book trading liquid names in five- and six-figure size has effectively none. That lets you hold capacity-constrained positions a $50B fund physically cannot touch. Small size is the advantage — not a handicap to be leveraged away.

What not to copy

Leverage

4–8× gross

Pods run it because they are factor-neutral with a professional risk overlay and prime-broker financing. Copying the leverage without the neutrality is how accounts die.

Short single names

unbounded loss

Borrow cost, squeeze risk, and no margin desk behind you. Use the bottom of the ranking as an avoid list instead — the backtest below says the same thing.

Latency signals

intraday

A fight lost by construction before you place the first order.

Deep ML on thin data

5y × 300 names

They have decades of tick data. On daily bars a large model memorises the regime; a small set of economically-motivated factors does not.

02

The production line, and this vault's version of it

Every serious desk runs the same six stages. QAE — the Quant Alpha Engine in scripts/qae.py — is one implementation of them, sized for a single operator with daily bars.

01DataPoint-in-time correctness. Look-ahead and survivorship manufacture alpha that does not exist.
02Alpha researchOne hypothesis, one measurable prediction, judged by rank IC.
03CombinationZ-score, winsorize, weight by measured information ratio — never by conviction.
04ConstructionRanks become positions: risk sizing, caps, neutrality, cost penalty.
05ExecutionAlpha is measured in basis points; so is slippage.
06Risk & attributionWhich factor, which name, which cost made the money.

Stage 1 — who gets into the universe

Caught by running it, not by reading it

The first live run put a 6.4% weight into a deal-pinned satellite name whose 63-day volatility had fallen to 14% against a 66% one-year figure — inverse-vol sizing loves a flatline. The guard above, plus a sizing-volatility floor at 60% of each name's own one-year volatility, was the fix. Removing that name and the decay ETFs raised every backtest metric.

Stage 3 — measuring a signal honestly

  1. Define the factor only on data available at time t.
  2. Cross-sectionally z-score within group — stocks against stocks, ETFs against ETFs — using median and MAD, winsorized at ±3. Pooling the two turns every volatility factor into a “you are an ETF” dummy.
  3. Compute the Spearman rank IC against forward group-relative returns at 5, 21 and 63 days, so the number measures selection, not market direction.
  4. Sample every 5 days for statistical power, then correct the t-statistic with Newey–West at lag = h/step. Overlapping windows inflate raw t by roughly √overlap; skipping this is the most common way a factor study lies to you.

Stages 4–6 — from a ranking to a book

  • Admission: mean t across the three horizons ≥ 1.0 and positive 21-day IC. A negative IC is never sign-flipped — that is fitting the regime, not measuring an effect.
  • Weights: 50% equal / 50% proportional to combined t. Pure t-weighting over-trusts five years of noise; pure equal-weighting throws away the only evidence there is.
  • Theme cap: at most 3 names per theme walking down the ranking.
  • Sizing: inverse volatility, single name capped at 8%, cap-and-redistribute so weights still sum to 100%.
  • Vol target: gross scaled so ex-ante portfolio volatility ≈ 10% annualized, remainder in cash, never levered.
  • Stops: 2.5 × ATR(20) below entry — every position risks the same amount of volatility, not the same percentage of price.

Book-level drawdown ladder

−5%Review: factor decay or ordinary noise? Cut gross exposure 25%.
−8%Cut gross 50%. No new positions outside the top decile.
−12%Flat the systematic book. Post-mortem before re-entry, then re-enter at half size.

The retail translation of the pod-shop ladder. Its whole value is that it is written down before the drawdown, not during one.

The result, stated without spin

QAE beat SPY on return and lost to it on Sharpe. Three things follow, and they are worth more than a flattering number would be. The test window was an exceptional bull run — a 1.46 Sharpe for a passive index is roughly double its long-run norm, so beating it risk-adjusted here is a very high bar. The short leg destroyed value (0.56 against 1.11 long-only), which is why the bottom decile is an avoid list rather than a short book. And the volatility-target overlay is the one construction change that genuinely improved the risk profile: realized 10.4% against a 10.0% target — the risk model is calibrated — with a −6.9% drawdown against SPY's −15.8%. It costs a great deal of return. Whether that trade is worth making is a preference, not a fact, but it is now measurable.

Biases still in these numbers

  • Survivorship / selection. The universe is the instrument list tracked today. Names dropped or delisted are absent. This flatters the result.
  • One regime. Roughly three years, almost entirely bull. Momentum's crash risk is not represented in it.
  • No borrow costs in the long/short line.
  • Two correlated factors. Effective breadth of one is the reason conviction on the composite sits at 2 out of 5, while conviction on the construction layer sits at 4.

This was the v1 read on a 5-year window. The tournament below re-ran the same signal inside complete systems on ten years of data, and the picture changed: with a vol target, the same momentum book beat SPY on Sharpe.

The tournament: 39 systems, ten years, one gate

Measuring factors one at a time found one honest signal. The next question is which system — signal, construction, risk overlay, diversifier — so 39 complete systems ran on the same days with the same costs, and a selection rule written before the results decided what counts.

The gate

A strategy is a winner only if it is top-quartile by Sharpe in 2017-09 → 2022-03 and beats SPY on Sharpe in 2022-03 → 2026-09, the half it was not selected on. Stock-picking strategies must also beat the equal weight of their own universe in that second half, because the universe is survivor-biased. Everything else is a candidate, not a result. Vol targeting is the biggest free lunch

same ten names

Unmanaged: Sharpe 0.99, drawdown −36%. Scaled to a 15% ex-ante vol: Sharpe 1.03, drawdown −18.6%, and the 2020 crash becomes −5.5%. Nothing about the selection changed.

Concentration paid

top-10 > top-20 > top-30

Sharpe 0.99 → 0.87 → 0.74. The signal lives in the tail of the ranking; names 11–30 add beta, not alpha.

Diversify across engines, not names

0.99 / 0.98 across halves

The blend has a lower Sharpe than the stock engine alone but a far steadier one, half the beta, and it was positive in 2022. Stability across regimes is what winning again requires.

Theme rotation failed

α −8% to −16% vs universe

Buying last season's hot theme ETF at 9–13× turnover lost in every variant. The tactical version of thematic investing does not work — the most useful negative result here.

What is still wrong with these numbers

Survivorship, quantified. The tracked universe's equal weight returned +25.2%/yr; the equal-weight S&P 500 ETF, which has no hindsight in it, returned +11.8%/yr. That ~13-point gap inflates every stock strategy's absolute return — only the α-vs-universe column is trusted, and the proposed system's +6.7%/yr survives it. Multiple testing. The best of 39 zero-skill strategies would beat SPY's Sharpe by about +1.01 through luck alone; the system's edge is +0.27. Nothing here clears that band. The split-sample gate is the mitigation; the defence is that both engines are the most-replicated effects in the literature. Caught mid-build: the realized-vol scaler was reading its own future curve. Its bugged ensemble had been a winner; it was not once fixed. Run everything twice.

Reference shelf

Books, in order

Chan, Quantitative Trading · Grinold & Kahn, Active Portfolio Management · Ilmanen, Expected Returns · Ang, Asset Management · López de Prado, Advances in Financial Machine Learning · Narang, Inside the Black Box.

Papers to read, not cite

Jegadeesh–Titman 1993 · Fama–French 1993 & 2015 · Carhart 1997 · Asness–Frazzini–Pedersen 2014 · Frazzini–Pedersen 2014 · Daniel–Moskowitz 2016 · McLean–Pontiff 2016 · Harvey–Liu–Zhu 2016.

Maths you actually need

Regression and its assumptions · covariance and correlation · z-scores and ranks · Spearman versus Pearson · t-stats and multiple testing · log returns · the variance of a weighted sum. That is genuinely most of it.

Skip until L4

Stochastic calculus, options pricing theory, deep learning. None of it helps a daily-bar cross-sectional book, and all of it is a procrastination magnet.

Three rules that outrank the syllabus

1. Write the invalidation before the entry. Every time.
2. Measure before you believe. A factor without an IC number is a story.
3. Your edge is size and patience, not speed or complexity.

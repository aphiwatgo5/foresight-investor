QAE — Quant Alpha Engine (methodology v1 → v2)

Definition: The vault's cross-sectional alpha layer. QAE ranks every liquid instrument
in the universe by a blend of price/volume factors whose weights are set by measured
predictive power (rank IC), then converts that ranking into a risk-sized book with a
volatility target, concentration caps, and pre-committed stops.

Scope. Decisions for the operator's own book, never advice to others. Every number
below is produced by scripts/qae.py from scripts/_hist/prices.parquet; nothing is
recalled from memory. Past performance ≠ future results.

Where it sits: strategy-sleeves answers which theme; foresight-factor-model
answers is this company good; mdaem-framework-v4 answers should I own it.
QAE answers a different question: given everything I can trade, what is the risk-optimal
ranked book today, and what is the measured evidence that the ranking predicts anything?

1. The six stages (mirrors quant-firm-anatomy §2)

Stage 1 — Universe

From ~330 tracked instruments, admit a name only if all hold on the decision date:

Filter Threshold Why
Asset class stock or ETF FX/crypto/futures have different return generating processes; ranking them against equities is a category error
History ≥ 400 trading days Every factor needs a 252d lookback
Liquidity median 63d dollar volume ≥ $3M Below this, the spread eats the edge
Price ≥ $3 Sub-$3 quotes are mostly spread
Structural decay UVXY/VXX/leveraged ETFs excluded Their return is a roll-cost artefact, not alpha; they would permanently occupy the bottom of any ranking
Vol-collapse guard 63d vol ≥ 40% of 252d vol A collapsed vol on a name that has risen is the signature of an announced acquisition. Momentum + near-zero vol makes a pinned deal stock look like the perfect position; it is dead money with deal-break tail risk.

The vol-collapse guard was added after the first live run put a 6.4% weight into a
deal-pinned satellite name purely because inverse-vol sizing loves a flatline. This is the
kind of failure only running the thing on real data surfaces.

Stage 2 — Factors

Fourteen price/volume factors, all defined so higher = better, all computable from data
available at time t:

mom_12_1 · mom_6_1 · accel · trend_q · rev_5 · rev_21 · lowvol · ivol ·
bab · dist_high · dist_low · vol_accum · skew · illiq

Each is cross-sectionally z-scored within its group (stocks against stocks, ETFs against
ETFs) using median/MAD rather than mean/std, and winsorized at ±3. Grouping matters: an
ETF is structurally lower-vol than a single stock, so a pooled z-score would turn every
volatility-linked factor into a "you are an ETF" dummy variable.

Stage 3 — IC measurement (the honest part)

For every factor and horizon h ∈ {5, 21, 63} trading days:
1. Sample every 5 trading days across the whole history.
2. Compute the Spearman rank IC between the factor z-score and the forward
group-relative return (each name's forward return minus its group's mean, so the
IC measures selection skill, not market direction).
3. Because 5-day sampling with a 21- or 63-day forward window overlaps, correct the
t-statistic with Newey–West at lag = h/step. Skipping this step is the single most
common way a factor study lies to you: overlapping windows inflate t by roughly √overlap.

Stage 4 — Weights

A factor is admitted only if:
- its mean t-stat across the three horizons ≥ 1.0 (the mean, not Stouffer's sum/√k —
the horizons are highly correlated, so the mean is the conservative bound), and
- its 21-day IC is positive.

We never flip a factor's sign to exploit a negative IC. A negative IC is information about
the regime, not a tradable inversion, and flipping it is regime-fitting.

Surviving factors are weighted 50% equal / 50% proportional to combined t. Pure
t-weighting over-trusts point estimates from 5 years of data; pure equal-weighting discards
the only evidence available. The 50/50 shrinkage is an explicit, documented prior.

Stage 5 — Portfolio construction

  1. Rank by composite score.
  2. Theme cap: at most 3 names per primary theme, walking down the ranking
    (portfolio-blueprint rule). Without it the top decile in a capex boom is one trade
    priced fifteen ways.
  3. Inverse-volatility sizing: w ∝ 1/σ, where σ is floored at 60% of the name's own
    1-year vol so a temporary vol collapse cannot inflate a weight.
  4. Single-name cap 8%, with cap-and-redistribute so the fully-invested weights still
    sum to 100%.
  5. Volatility target: scale gross exposure so ex-ante portfolio vol ≈ 10% annualized,
    using σ_p ≈ √(Σwᵢ²σᵢ² + ρ·ΣΣwᵢwⱼσᵢσⱼ) with ρ = 0.50. The remainder sits in cash.
    No leverage is ever applied — gross is capped at 100%.

Stage 6 — Risk

  • Stop: 2.5 × ATR(20) below entry, so every position risks a comparable amount of
    volatility, not a comparable percentage of price.
  • Invalidation: the name leaves the top quartile of the composite at the next rebalance.
  • Book-level ladder: −5% → cut gross 25%; −8% → cut 50%; −12% → flat and post-mortem
    (the retail translation of the pod-shop stop ladder, quant-firm-anatomy §1).
  • Rebalance: every 21 trading days, trading only the deltas.
  • Costs: 10 bps per side modelled explicitly in every backtest number.

2. What the measurement actually said (run of 2026-08-29, data through 2026-08-27)

Only 2 of 14 factors cleared the bar. That is the headline, and it should be.

Factor IC 21d NW t Verdict
mom_12_1 (12-1 momentum) +0.030 +1.2 admitted, 49.8% weight
trend_q (126d trend quality) +0.025 +1.0 admitted, 50.2% weight
accel +0.021 +1.0 positive but below the bar
dist_high · illiq · vol_accum +0.013 · +0.011 · +0.008 +0.5 → +0.7 measured, not admitted
mom_6_1 · rev_5 · skew · ivol −0.004 → −0.013 −0.6 → −0.2 negative IC — dropped
rev_21 · lowvol · dist_low · bab −0.037 · −0.040 · −0.049 · −0.051 −1.8 → −1.0 negative IC — dropped, not flipped

Three readings worth keeping:
1. Momentum is the only thing that survives on this data. Both admitted factors are
momentum in different clothes, and their correlation is high — so the composite's
effective factor breadth is ~1, not 2. That is a real weakness of v1.
2. The low-volatility and betting-against-beta anomalies are inverted in this sample
(lowvol IC −0.040, bab IC −0.051). Over 2021–2026 the high-beta, high-vol megacap
growth complex led the market. This is a regime fact, not a broken factor — and it is
precisely why we do not flip signs on negative IC.
3. The t-stats are weak in absolute terms. With 14 factors tested, a multiple-testing
correction (Harvey–Liu–Zhu) would demand t ≈ 3, not 1. Nothing here clears that bar.
The composite is used because momentum is one of the most-replicated effects in the
literature and its sign here matches theory — not because this study proved it.

Backtest (walk-forward weights, 35 monthly rebalances ≈ 3 years, costed)

Book CAGR Vol Sharpe MaxDD
Long/short decile (20×20) +24.6% 44.1% 0.56 −31.4%
Long-only top-20, equal weight +63.4% 51.5% 1.23 −38.0%
Long-only top-20, inverse-vol +44.0% 39.7% 1.11 −32.4%
… + theme cap (max 3/theme) +40.1% 37.4% 1.07 −32.8%
… + 10% vol target (cash buffer) +13.1% 10.4% 1.26 −6.9%
SPY (same window) +21.9% 15.0% 1.46 −15.8%

The honest conclusion: QAE beat SPY on return but not on Sharpe over this window.
Three things follow from that, and they are more valuable than a flattering number:
- The test window (≈2023-08 → 2026-08) was an exceptional bull run for SPY — a 1.46 Sharpe
for a passive index is roughly double its long-run norm. Beating it risk-adjusted in
this window is a very high bar.
- The short leg destroys value in a bull market. Long/short Sharpe 0.56 vs long-only
1.11. Confirms the quant-firm-anatomy §5 rule: for a solo operator without a
professional risk overlay, the bottom decile is an avoid/trim list, not a short book.
- The vol-target overlay is the one construction change that actually improved the risk
profile: realized vol 10.4% against a 10.0% target (the risk model is calibrated) and a
−6.9% max drawdown against SPY's −15.8%. It costs a lot of return. Whether that trade is
worth it is a preference, not a fact — but it is now measurable.

Known biases in the backtest (all disclosed, none fixed in v1):
- Survivorship / selection: the universe is the list of instruments tracked today.
Names that were dropped or delisted are absent. This flatters results.
- Test window is short and one-regime (~3y, mostly bull). Momentum's crash risk
(Daniel–Moskowitz 2016) is not represented in it.
- No borrow costs in the long/short line.
- No point-in-time fundamentals — QAE is deliberately price/volume-only, so this bias
does not apply here, but it is why fundamental factors stayed out of v1.

3. How to run it

scripts/.venv/bin/python scripts/fetch_history.py --years 5 --no-upgrades   # refresh prices
scripts/.venv/bin/python scripts/qae.py                                    # full: IC + backtest + book
scripts/.venv/bin/python scripts/qae.py --no-backtest                       # fast: IC + book only (~6s)

Outputs land in scripts/_hist/: qae_ic.json (factor evidence), qae_backtest.json
(walk-forward results), qae_book.json (today's sized book), qae_report.md (the report
that feeds the wiki synthesis).

3b. v2 — the tournament-selected system (2026-09-06)

v1 measured factors. v2 ran 39 complete systems (signal + construction + risk overlay +
diversifier) over 10 years with a pre-registered split-sample gate — see
strategy-tournament-2026-09-06. The result replaces the v1 book construction:

Two engines, 50/50, rebalanced every 21 trading days.
- Engine A — Momentum-10, vol-targeted. v1 composite (0.5 × z(12-1 momentum) + 0.5 × z(trend
quality)), top 10 (not 15–20), max 3 per theme, inverse-vol capped at 8%, whole leg scaled to a
15% ex-ante vol target, remainder in T-bills. Backtest 2017-09 → 2026-09: Sharpe 1.03,
max DD −18.6%, 2020 crash −5.5%, 2022 bear −0.4%.
- Engine B — Dual momentum with a defensive-momentum leg. Hold the best 12m performer of
SPY/QQQ/INDA/GLD/DBC/VNQ if it beats T-bills; else the best 12m of IEF/TLT/GLD/BIL.
- Blend: CAGR +18.2% · vol 15.7% · Sharpe 0.98 · max DD −23.0% · β 0.51 · +9.6% in 2022
(SPY −18.2%) · half-1/half-2 Sharpe 0.99/0.98 · survives 25 bps costs (0.94).
SPY over the same days: +15.1% · 18.8% · 0.71 · −33.7%.

Three construction lessons that changed the spec: vol targeting was the biggest single
improvement (same names, drawdown −36% → −19%); concentration paid (top-10 > top-20 > top-30);
a second engine stabilised the Sharpe across halves more than any tweak to the first.
Three things that failed and were removed from consideration: theme-ETF rotation (negative alpha),
the 200-dma regime gate (whipsaw), and a walk-forward ridge model (no better than the hand blend).

Caveats carried forward, unchanged: the +0.27 Sharpe edge sits inside a +1.01 multiple-testing
luck band; the stock universe is survivor-biased (~13 pts/yr vs RSP) so only the α-vs-EW-universe
column (+6.7%/yr) is trusted; the momentum crash tail is untested in this window.

4. Our view (v1)

Conviction 2/5 on the composite as a standalone return engine — the evidence is weak
(t ≈ 1.2 on the best factor) and effective breadth is ~1 factor.
Conviction 3/5 on the v2 two-engine system — it passed a split-sample gate and a cost
stress, and both engines are the most-replicated effects in the literature; it did not clear the
luck band.
Conviction 4/5 on the construction layer: vol targeting, theme caps, vol-floored
inverse-vol sizing and ATR stops each demonstrably improved the risk profile, and they will
keep doing so regardless of which signal feeds them.

Invalidation for v1: if mom_12_1 IC turns negative over a rolling 12-month window
(measured at the monthly re-run), the composite is switched off and the book goes to the
strategy-sleeves default until a new factor set clears the bar.

v2 backlog, in priority order
1. Fix effective breadth. Two correlated momentum factors is one bet. Add genuinely
orthogonal alphas — earnings-announcement drift, residual (beta-neutral) reversal,
analyst-revision momentum from the existing _hist/upgrades/ event logs.
2. Orthogonalize the composite against market beta and theme dummies so the ranking
is not just "own more semis."
3. Regime-conditional weights — measure IC separately in SPY>200dma vs <200dma regimes
rather than assuming one weight set works in both.
4. Extend the window to 7y to capture at least one real drawdown (the standing v4
backlog item, still open).
5. Point-in-time fundamentals (SEC EDGAR) to let value/quality factors into the IC study
without look-ahead.
